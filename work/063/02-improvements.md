## 3 ways FOR
1. **Use the fail-closed edge rule, not the 120-day bar, and change the guardrail fixture to match.** 01 § Numbers: the leads are BAC/USB 100 d, CSX 107 d and VALE 113 d, and all 21 are under 120. So request § Guardrail 1 as written ("data start to first earnings date > EARNINGS_GAP_WARN days") flags none of the real cases. A fixture with a lead over 120 d (Guardrail 2) would pass under that rule while BAC still sells silently, which is the RI-001 pattern. Improvement: make "before the first listed date" unknown outright (01 open question 1, option a). Then add a second fixture whose lead is between one quarter and 120 d (for example 100 d, the BAC case). The 100-day fixture is what fails under a threshold-only rule. The mutation in 05/06 should run against both fixtures. Reasoning chain: quarterly spacing is 90-92 d (01 finding 8), so any lead over about 90 d can hide a report, and 100-113 d leads did hide one (01 findings 10-12).
2. **Make the edge check the mirror of the trailing check already in `_earnings_known`, so one mutation covers it.** Today it is `bool(td.earnings) and td.earnings[-1] >= d` (L372-374), the trailing edge only. Under option (a) the leading edge needs one more clause, `td.earnings[0] <= d`. That keeps the rule in one function that `simulate` already calls, gives the same `EARNINGS_UNKNOWN` code the bot uses (RI-002 entry: MISSING_POLICY `EARNINGS_UNKNOWN` for blank `next_earnings`), and the i3 mutation can delete exactly that clause. The warning still has to be computed in `simulate` against the first sale day, because `load_ticker` gets no `start` (01 finding 4). The value is the lead in days (e.g. "earnings: first listed date 2020-04-15, 100 days after first quote day 2020-01-06; sales before it blocked (EARNINGS_UNKNOWN)"). It goes through `df.attrs` into `summarize` "warnings", so it reaches `reports/real/summary.json` next to the existing gap warning (01 finding 5). That meets request § What we want ("gets a warning in reports/real/").
3. **The cost is small and limited to research, and the risk it removes is the whole position at this account size.** The live bot is not touched (01 § Fit: it uses snapshot `next_earnings` with MISSING_POLICY). At most 5 sales change among the 17 reported tickers (01 findings 15-16: 2 BAC, 1 USB, 2 CSX), plus each other ticker's first 16-52 days. On a 1-lot ~$3k account, one call held through an unlisted report is the whole position, and the CSX 2020-01-06 call was in fact assigned (01 finding 15). A backtest that scores that risk as zero overstates the strategy, and the fix makes the score honest at little cost. A refinement to "Done looks like": I2 should report the measured count under the new rule and not expect "about 247". That figure came from 061's lookback rule (01 finding 17; audit L40-41 shows OXY and CSX shifts the edge rule would not produce).

## 3 ways AGAINST
1. **Fail closed removes the early-2020 crash months for BAC, CSX and USB, so the test sample shifts for a data reason, not a market reason.** Under option (a), BAC and USB cannot sell before 2020-04-15, and CSX not before 04-22 (01 finding 7). That drops their calls over Feb-Mar 2020, including the 02-21 to 03-20 sales in 01 finding 16 that held no hidden report. Those are stress-period outcomes. A-rows that compare covered calls against buy-and-hold may move because a hard period was dropped for 3 of 17 stocks, not because of the strategy. Reasoning chain: fewer stress-period trades for those tickers, but buy-and-hold still runs over the same days (simulate starts both arms at `days[0]`, L405-406), so the comparison for those stocks includes a stretch where the covered-call arm is forced to hold stock only. Mitigation (for R3): the warning and 05 must say that those days are "stock only, no sale (earnings unknown)". 05 must also show the A-row change per ticker, if any, so the person does not read it as a strategy result.
2. **It also blocks sales that are probably fine, and every one of the 21 tickers loses its first days.** Option (a) blocks all days before the first listed date, even where the lead (16-52 d, 01 § Numbers) is too short to hide a quarter. OXY (52 d) and PBR (44 d) lose sales whose lives may hold no report at all (OXY's Q4 2019 date is UNVERIFIED, 01 finding 13 and open question 5). Option (b) (lead > the ticker's shortest listed interval) keeps those sales, but it is a labelled heuristic that infers "no report" from cadence. That is the kind of filled-in value RI-002 warns against. Either way the person loses sample or accepts a guess, and R3 has to state which one and why.
3. **It makes the edge strict and leaves interior gaps lax, and it moves several existing tests.** After 063, a day before the first date blocks, but a day inside an interior gap over 120 d (CMCSA, MDLZ, MO, T, VZ; 01 open question 4) still only warns and sells. The reports would treat two kinds of the same data hole differently, and "earnings holes handled" would be easy to over-read. Also, `_tiny` fixtures (lead 151 d) and the control sales in `test_earnings_today_blocks`, `test_take50_only_in_first_third`, `test_sales_under_3_cents_are_counted_not_blocked` and L380 become EARNINGS_UNKNOWN. `REAL_BAC`'s pinned 24 calls / 20 rejections also change (01 open question 3). Editing fixtures to add a prior date can look like weakening tests. I1 must list each edit as "fixture gains a prior earnings date so the control stays a control", with the assertion itself unchanged, and I3 must check that no assertion was loosened. R3 should write the interior-gap asymmetry as out of scope, possibly with a follow-up item.
