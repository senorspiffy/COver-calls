## 3 ways FOR
1. **Change the person's exit rule: with this account size, hold to expiry by default and use 50% only as an early-exit exception.** ORATS backtested covered calls on 16 large-cap S&P 500 stocks back to 2007 (about 3.5 million parameter combinations). It found "Holding to expiration was better than exiting after profit". The profit target ORATS tested was 75%, not 50%, so this is indirect evidence against early exit, not a test of 50%. [https://orats.com/blog/covered-call-backtest-finding-the-best-maturity-strike-iv-and-earnings-methods] Reasoning that supports it at $3k: the 50% exit adds a $0.65 buy-back fee per cycle (R1 N3, N4), and after the buy-back the shares are uncapped and unhedged until a new call is sold (R1 D4). Suggested middle ground (reasoning, not tested): close early only if 50% is reached in the first ~third of the term (premium decayed much faster than time), then sell the next call the same day.
2. **About 30 days, out-of-the-money (≈10-30 delta), not weekly or 0DTE, and skip earnings.** ORATS: best DTE was 30, best delta was 0.10, and "Avoiding earnings was better than trading through earnings". It also found selling calls when IV percentile was above 66% performed best. [ORATS, same URL] This agrees with R1's index evidence that 30-delta BXMD beat ATM BXM (R1 B9, B10) and with Chinese-language broker guidance that the usual choice is a "近期深度虚值（OTM）" (near-term, far out-of-the-money) call on stocks you are "略微看涨至中性" (slightly bullish to neutral) on. [https://www.moomoo.com/us/hans/learn/covered-calls-strategy] Note: 10 delta pays little premium; on a ~$28 stock that may be only a few cents and the $0.65 fee will eat a large share (R1 N3). In practice 20-30 delta is the compromise at this size (reasoning; R1 E2).
3. **Use it in the market it suits: flat to slightly up, on a stock you would hold anyway.** Commerzbank (German): best suited to "Stabile bis leicht positive Notierungen: Es bestehen verhältnismäßig gute Chancen, dass die Optionen wertlos verfallen." [https://www.commerzbank.de/investieren/wissen/covered-call-writing/] Same idea in R1 A4 (Fidelity). FlashAlpha's SPY/QQQ backtest also found "The calls paid in 2022, when both ETFs fell". [https://flashalpha.com/articles/do-covered-calls-beat-holding-shares-spy-qqq-backtest] So the strategy does what it is supposed to (cushion, income) in sideways or mildly down years.

## 3 ways AGAINST
1. **In rising markets it lags simply holding the shares, in every tested setup.** FlashAlpha tested SPY and QQQ from 3 Jan 2022 to 19 Oct 2026 across deltas 0.10-0.50 and 1-week to 3-month expiries (48 setups). Covered calls trailed buy-and-hold in all 48, by $774 to $16,988 per 100 shares: "The calls paid in 2022, when both ETFs fell, and cost money in every rally since." [https://flashalpha.com/articles/do-covered-calls-beat-holding-shares-spy-qqq-backtest] Consistent with R1 B5/B6 (BXM about 6.1%/yr vs S&P 500 about 11.0%/yr, 2006-2025). Caveat: FlashAlpha bought back at expiry (no 50% rule) and the period was mostly a bull market.
2. **The downside is almost all still there; the premium is a thin cushion.** Monex (Japanese): "株価が大きく下落した場合は、プレミアム収入だけでは損失を相殺できないリスクもあります。" [https://media.monex.co.jp/ud/glossary/678f1a30b57622c5a7000009] KB (Korean): gains are capped, "기초자산 하락에 따른 손실은 그대로 반영돼". [https://kbthink.com/etf/covered-call.html] Economipedia (Spanish) even calls it "una estrategia con mucho riesgo" because losses are only limited when the stock reaches zero. [https://economipedia.com/definiciones/call-cubierta.html] With $3,000 in one stock (R1 N1), a 20% drop costs about −$510 versus ~$49/month of premium (R1 N2): roughly 10 months of premium wiped out.
3. **Small, frequent trades lose money to fees and taxes; "steady income" is not "steady return".** Commerzbank lists the "vergleichsweise geringer Gewinn durch die Optionsprämie" (comparatively small gain from the premium) as a drawback. [Commerzbank, above] At $3k, weekly round trips cost ~2.25%/yr in commissions and 0DTE ~5.5% (R1 N4), and calls of 30 days or less can't be qualified covered calls, so gains are short-term (R1 K2, K5). The 50% rule doubles the fee count per cycle (R1 N3). R1 N7: ~20%/yr gross premium on BXM still produced only ~6%/yr total return.

## Holes to watch out for (summary)
1. **Big drop in the one stock** wipes out many months of premium (Monex, KB, moomoo, Economipedia; R1 N2). Have a rule for when to stop writing calls and exit the stock (none sourced: house rule, UNVERIFIED).
2. **Bull markets**: you will lag holding the shares (FlashAlpha 48/48 setups; R1 B5). Decide in advance whether income or total return is the goal.
3. **Getting called away on a big rally** (JPX: must sell at the strike). Only write on shares you are happy to sell at that strike (R1 F4).
4. **The gap after a 50% buy-back**: shares are uncapped and unhedged until the next call is sold (R1 D4).
5. **Fees on small premiums**: at $0.65/contract, a $0.10 premium loses ~26% of its 50% gain to fees (R1 N3). The 50% rule doubles fee events.
6. **Earnings and high-volatility names**: ORATS found avoiding earnings better; moomoo warns high-vol stocks can cause major losses.
7. **Ex-dividend early assignment** when the call is in the money (R1 F3).
8. **Taxes**: ≤30-day calls are never qualified covered calls; gains are short-term (R1 K2, K5).
9. **Backtest limits**: ORATS is a vendor's promotional piece with a past-performance disclaimer; it tested 75% (not 50%) and large caps, not sub-$30 stocks. FlashAlpha covers one mostly-bullish period. Treat both as directional, not proof.
10. **Still UNVERIFIED after R2**: a direct test of the 50% rule on covered calls; tastytrade 21-DTE data; roll-for-credit rule; covered-call ETF fees.
