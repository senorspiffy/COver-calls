3. **Make the "learning bot" an honest, pre-registered rule tester with walk-forward selection, led by an IV-level filter.** The one timing filter with any covered-call evidence is IV level: ORATS found best results with IV percentile > 66% (R1 C5, 001 verified), and Montréal Exchange (French) says « En période de grande incertitude (et de forte volatilité), un investisseur averti peut générer des revenus » [https://www.m-x.ca/f_publications_fr/covered_calls_managing_risk_exposure_and_generating_value_fr.pdf]. Full ML can't be trained on ~120 monthly trades (R1 D4), and the best ML only explains a fraction of a percent of monthly stock returns (R1 D1, R² 0.33–0.40%). Overfitting is the main danger (R1 D3, Bailey et al. PBO). A Russian Habr post on robot optimisation names the trap in its title, «эффективный способ наторговать миллион задним числом», and advises that «Отыскание координат глобального максимума / минимума ЦФ зачастую не является целью оптимизации» and to check «насколько робот чувствителен к изменениям настроек» [https://habr.com/post/430134/] (AndreySitaev, 16 Nov 2018). So: a fixed list of variants (baseline 001, IV-pct filter, up-day entry, weekday, weekly), each chosen walk-forward on trailing years, all reported, with a parameter-sensitivity table and a held-out final period. The person's "day-to-day action" comes from the bot's **daily scan and report** (IV percentile, spread, earnings/ex-div dates, "would the rule trade today?"), not from trading more often.

## 3 ways AGAINST
1. **"Sell into strength" (sell after green days) has no support, and the nearest evidence points the other way.** A secondary write-up of a tastylive backtest (16-delta *strangles*, ~45 DTE closed at 21 DTE, ~13 years, ~3,500–4,000 trades) says entries "made on 3% or greater down days" more than doubled average P&L vs any-day entries, while "Win rates remained broadly stable across all entry thresholds, sitting in the 72% to 74% range", and the largest loss grew with deeper down-day entries [https://stockwirex.com/?p=48767] (Ryan Dhillon, 3 Oct 2026; marketing author; original tastylive data not linked, so treat as UNVERIFIED primary). This fits R1 C1 (IV falls after up days, so a fixed-delta call pays less). Chinese broker guidance points the same way for covered-call holders: «波动率上升为负面，下降为正面。» [https://finance.sina.cn/option/knowledge/2020-01-13/detail-iihnzahk2953598.d.html]: selling right after a rise sells at lower IV. The Japan Exchange's covered-call timing column talks about *trend*, not days: «上昇トレンド、またはあまり動きのない場合にコールを売る。» and «原則として、取引最終日まで2週間以上の日数を残して売る» [https://www.jpx.co.jp/ose-toshijuku/column/kabuop_practicalQA08.html]. AQR adds that covered calls already carry an "unintended" market-timing bet: "over a quarter of a covered call's risk may be attributed to equity timing" [https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/White-Papers/Covered-Calls-and-Their-Unintended-Reversal-Bet] (AQR, 2 Jun 2014). A daily timing rule adds more timing risk on top of that. **Keep it only as one pre-registered backtest variant; don't build it into the bot's default.**
2. **The fixes for the ≤ $28 problem each bring their own risk; none is free.**
   - *Single stocks* (SOFI/F/T/PFE) add earnings gaps (ORATS: avoid earnings; with quarterly reports, about 4 of 12 monthly cycles need an earlier expiry or a skip, reasoning), ex-dividend early assignment (001 F3; F, T and PFE pay dividends, UNVERIFIED current amounts), and one-company risk. 001 step 9a caps a single stock at 50% of capital, so layout (a) is exactly at that limit and (b)/(c) break it. Rankia: «el subyacente puede caer mucho. La prima no te protege más allá de su importe.» and «Cada contrato obliga sobre 100 acciones: para una acción de 50 €, unos 5.000 € reservados por contrato.» [https://www.rankia.com/blog/opciones-desde-cero/6011492-rueda-wheel-strategy-opciones-financieras]. SOFI's tight spread comes with high volatility (house view; its IV wasn't read today).
   - *KWEB*: concentrated China exposure (R1 Fit).
   - *Poor man's covered call* is not a $3k fix on Webull. It needs Level 3, a margin account and ≥ $2,000 margin equity (001 G4), and the long call can go to zero. Saxo Italia: «Le opzioni acquistate possono scadere senza valore, con la conseguente perdita dell'investimento iniziale» and «Se la call short entra in-the-money, i trader potrebbero dover effettuare il rolling della posizione» [https://www.home.saxo/it-it/learn/guides/options/understanding-the-poor-mans-covered-call]. Spreads on 6–12-month calls for these names were not checked (UNVERIFIED; likely wider than weeklies).
   - *Covered-call ETF* (001 alt. 2) removes the bot's purpose.
3. **A bot built for "action" on a $3k account costs more than it can earn, and Webull's API has open gaps.** Every extra fill costs half the spread (001 table: weekly + 50% buy-back at $0.05 spread = $260/yr = 8.7% of $3k), so a bot that trades more to feel active can lose more than the timing can earn. On the API side: sell-to-open is DAY-limit only (R1 A4; QuantConnect agrees); no chain endpoint found (R1 A7); real-time options data needs a separate paid OPRA OpenAPI entitlement, while the sandbox has 15-minute-delayed data (R1 A9, A12). The sources also **disagree on paper trading**: QuantConnect says "The Webull API doesn't support paper trading" [QC Webull page above], but Webull's docs describe a "Sandbox (paper trading)" (R1 A11). Webull's 21 Jul 2026 press release announces "OpenAPI access for paperTrade" and simulated trading in "stocks, options, …" but does not say the OpenAPI paper access covers options [https://www.webull.com.au/news-detail/15265422808409089]. Whether options orders work in the sandbox is UNVERIFIED. So phase 2 should log "would-be" fills in our own paper ledger from quotes (no reliance on the Webull sandbox to fill options), and phase 3 stays research-only.

## Holes to watch out for (summary)
1. **Ticker universe (biggest change).** 001 step 1 ("diversified ETF ≤ $28") can't be met (R1 E2). Choose one: (a) SOFI shares + F cash-secured put at $3k; (b) 200 F, 100 T or KWEB alone (breaks 001's 50% single-stock cap unless KWEB); (c) save to ~$5.3k for two names (e.g. T + PFE); (d) covered-call ETF. Person must pick (R1 open Q7). The backtester should take the ticker as a parameter and screen by spread/OI, not by "ETF".
2. **Today's quotes are weeklies.** 30–45 DTE monthly spreads for SOFI/F/T/PFE/KWEB are unchecked; monthlies are often wider than the nearest weekly (UNVERIFIED). The paper bot's first job: log real monthly spreads daily.
3. **Earnings and ex-dividend dates** become mandatory inputs once single stocks are allowed (ORATS; 001 F3). The bot needs an earnings/dividend calendar source. None was found in Webull's OpenAPI index (UNVERIFIED).
4. **Up-day timing may hurt.** It sells at lower IV (Sina; R1 C1), and the only quantified data (secondary, strangles) favours down days. Test it as one variant only.
5. **IV-rank entry has its own trap.** High IV right after a crash can go higher (Italian source). IV percentile needs a year of IV history per ticker: QuantConnect data, or the bot's own logs. VIX can't serve for SOFI/F/T/PFE/KWEB (R1 B11).
6. **Overfitting.** With ~120 monthly trades per ticker, show every variant, its walk-forward result and a sensitivity table (Bailey PBO; Habr). No ML model in v1.
7. **Webull API gaps.** Sell-side DAY only; no chain endpoint; separate paid OPRA entitlement; paper trading for options through the OpenAPI is contested (QC says no; Webull says sandbox/paperTrade). Keep the paper ledger our own. No live orders without the person's explicit go-ahead.
8. **Weekly cadence.** JPX advises ≥ 2 weeks to expiry; 001 allows weekly only after logged spreads ≤ $0.02. SOFI and F's $0.01 spreads could meet that, so weekly becomes testable for those names (a change from 001, which had no such ticker).
9. **PMCC** needs Level 3 + margin + $2k margin equity on Webull. The long call can go to zero. Not a $3k starting fix.
10. **Still UNVERIFIED:** QuantConnect free-tier limits and reachability from this container; Webull sandbox options support; monthly spreads for the new tickers; current dividends/earnings dates for F/T/PFE/SOFI; the original tastylive down-day data.
